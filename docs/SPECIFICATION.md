## 1. Problem Statement & Regulatory Context

Under the Administrative Procedure Act (5 U.S.C. § 553) and global equivalents, federal and state agencies must publish a **Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (NPRM)** and invite public comment. However, traditional rulemaking comment portals face catastrophic failures:

1. **Astroturfed Lobbying Floods:** Industry front groups submit hundreds of thousands of form-letter variants generated by automated bots, drowning out genuine public and small-business concerns.
2. **Administrative Confirmation Bias:** Regulatory agencies cherry-pick supportive submissions for oral testimony hearings, marginalizing rigorous dissenting studies.
3. **Black-Box Witness Selection:** Hearing witnesses are chosen through opaque backroom negotiations rather than merit-based impact coverage.

The **Regulatory Docket Allocator** implements an on-chain, verifiable administrative sortition protocol using GenLayer's GenVM:
- Every public brief or scientific study receives a verifiable **APA Compliance Receipt**.
- Submissions are frozen in a cryptographically committed manifest.
- Non-deterministic LLM clustering identifies distinct **Substantive Regulatory Impact Vectors** without hallucination, validated across validator nodes via the **Equivalence Principle**.
- A deterministic **Balanced Witness Sortition Algorithm** empanels oral hearing witnesses covering 100% of substantive impact domains.
- A decentralized **Evidence Contestation Window** allows the public to challenge web content drift or astroturfing collusion, arbitrated on-chain via Dragon consensus.

## 2. Finite State Machine (Lifecycle Stages)

A regulatory docket traverses seven discrete, monotonic states:

```
[0. ENROLLING] 
      │  Agency enrolls public submissions & scientific briefs.
      │  Receipt issued: SHA256(DocketID | SubID | URL | Digest | Registrar)
      ▼
[1. MANIFEST_LOCKED] 
      │  Manifest sealed. Computed SHA-256 == ExpectedManifestDigest.
      │  No further submissions or alterations permitted.
      ▼
[2. IMPACT_CONSENSUS] 
      │  GenVM Dragon consensus derives 1 to 6 Regulatory Impact Clusters.
      │  Equivalence Principle asserts partition invariance.
      ▼
[3. WITNESSES_ALLOCATED] 
      │  Deterministic balanced sortition empanels oral hearing witnesses.
      │  Coverage-first guarantee ensures every impact domain is voiced.
      ▼
[4. CONTESTATION_OPEN] 
      │  Active window for bonded administrative evidence challenges.
      │  (PROVENANCE_MISMATCH or DUPLICATE_ASTROTURF)
      ▼
[5. SOVEREIGN_RATIFIED] 
      │  Contestation deadline expires; all challenges resolved.
      │  Docket finalized. Hearing witness roll permanently immutable.
      ▼
(Optional: ANNULLED_PRELOCK) 
         Organizer can abort pre-lock if charter errata is detected.
```

## 3. Mathematical Model of Balanced Witness Sortition

### Invariants
Let:
- $\mathcal{S} = \{s_1, s_2, \dots, s_N\}$ be the set of enrolled public submissions.
- $\mathcal{I} = \{i_1, i_2, \dots, i_K\}$ be the set of derived regulatory impact clusters ($1 \le K \le 6$).
- $W \in \mathbb{N}$ be the total witness slot capacity ($1 \le W \le 50$).
- $R(s) \in [1, 100]$ be the evaluated substantive regulatory relevance score.
- $H(s) \in \{0, 1\}^{256}$ be the cryptographic SHA-256 digest of submission $s$.

### Two-Pass Selection Algorithm
1. **Pass 1 (Impact Domain Coverage):**
   For each regulatory impact domain $i_k \in \mathcal{I}$, identify the top-ranked submission:
   $$s^*_k = \arg\max_{s \in i_k, \text{eligible}(s)} \left( R(s) \cdot 2^{256} - H(s) \right)$$
   If $K \ge W$, empanel the top $W$ representatives among $\{s^*_k\}$.
   If $K < W$, empanel all $K$ domain seed representatives.

2. **Pass 2 (Proportional Depth):**
   Fill remaining $W - K$ witness seats with the highest-ranked candidates across the entire docket, subject to a maximum cap of 3 witnesses per impact domain.

3. **Deterministic Tie-Breaking:**
   Ties in relevance score are resolved deterministically by lower lexicographical SHA-256 hash:
   $$\text{TieBreakKey}(s) = \left( -R(s), \; H(s), \; \text{index}(s) \right)$$
